Why our offline image sync cannot keep pace, and what replaces it

Our clusters run OpenShift 4.18 on the EUS channel and are well behind. Standard maintenance for 4.18 ended on 25 August 2026, and the plan is to move to 4.20 and then 4.22. Every step waits for someone to copy images and catalogs into the local registry by hand. That sync has failed several times, and Red Hat has replaced the tools it relies on.

Every fix has to cross the gap by hand

The left lane is measured from Red Hat's public endpoints. The right lane is our process as the team describes it.

The road from 4.18 to 4.22

Between EUS releases the control plane passes through the odd minor while the workers stay paused. That makes four release payloads, and under today's process each one needs a sync before its hop can start.

A conditional edge is not a blocked one. The six risks declared on the 4.21 to 4.22 edges include one that applies to every cluster (kube-state-metrics crash loops when CronJobs set a time zone) and others limited to multi-arch, s390x, Portworx or third-party CNI clusters. A disconnected cluster sees those risks only if its local update service has current graph data.

Whichever tool our sync uses today, Red Hat's 4.22 documentation has moved on from it. Staying disconnected means migrating the process as well as running it.

What the proposal removes, and what stays

Neither design makes the remaining work go away. The updates still have to be run, the clusters still have to be pointed at an update service and a registry, and the operator catalog still has to be gated through GitOps so that only approved items are exposed. That work exists today and in both designs.

The team's assessment is that our backlog of fixes and our limits on upgrading come from this process, not from OpenShift. The measurements are consistent with that: Red Hat has shipped 156 fixes for 4.18 this year, and its update graph recommends a path to 4.20 from every 4.18 release. What is missing is the content inside our network.

The rows on DNS, signatures, catalogs and the Quay failure KBs come from the plan's research of 2026-09-25. That research found no Red Hat document that requires Consulting for Quay; bringing them in is our choice.

Still to settle

  • Security has not yet said whether cluster nodes may reach Red Hat through the proxy. The preferred design depends on that answer.
  • Order of work: the cluster-wide proxy is a documented configuration on 4.18. Enabling it before the updates would let the hops to 4.20 and 4.22 run without any sync. Red Hat Support should confirm that removing the mirror sets also removes the signature acknowledgment before 4.21.
  • The Red Hat Consulting engagement and the Quay specialist for the alternative are not arranged.
  • This page does not record which 4.18 release each cluster is on, which tool the current sync uses, or how many times it has failed. Those three facts would make the case stronger.
  • The plan also holds a third design that sits between these two: update graph and signatures from Red Hat through the proxy, images from the registry we run today. Red Hat does not document that combination.
